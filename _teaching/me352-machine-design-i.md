## Course Overview

ME 352 introduces the systematic design of mechanical components using mechanics of materials, failure theories, material selection, safety factors, and engineering standards. Students learn to translate functional requirements and loading conditions into safe, reliable, and manufacturable designs.

## Course Content

- The mechanical design process
- Materials and manufacturing considerations
- Stress, strain, and deformation in machine components
- Stress concentration and notch sensitivity
- Static failure theories
- Design for strength and stiffness
- Bolted and threaded joints
- Welded joints
- Mechanical springs
- Introduction to fatigue design

## Course Learning Outcomes

Upon successful completion of the course, students should be able to:

1. Apply a structured engineering design process.
2. Determine stresses and deformations in mechanical components.
3. Select appropriate materials and safety factors.
4. Apply static-failure theories and account for stress concentrations.
5. Design bolted, welded, and spring components.
6. Evaluate designs for safety, reliability, and manufacturability.

## Teaching and Learning Approach

The course combines clear presentation of fundamental principles with worked engineering examples, structured problem solving, and design or analysis activities appropriate to the subject. Emphasis is placed on connecting mathematical formulations to physical behavior, checking the reasonableness of results, and communicating engineering decisions clearly.

## Assessment Methods

- Homework and design exercises
- Quizzes
- Midterm examination
- Component-design assignments
- Final examination

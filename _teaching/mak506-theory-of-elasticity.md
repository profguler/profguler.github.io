## Course Overview

MAK 506 develops the mathematical foundations of linear elasticity and their application to classical boundary-value problems. The course emphasizes tensor notation, constitutive relations, equilibrium and compatibility, two-dimensional formulations, and analytical solution techniques.

## Course Content

- Stress and strain tensors
- Coordinate transformation and principal values
- Constitutive equations for linear elastic solids
- Equilibrium, compatibility, and boundary conditions
- Navier equations and uniqueness
- Plane stress and plane strain
- Airy stress functions
- Polar-coordinate formulations
- Torsion and selected three-dimensional problems
- Energy methods in elasticity

## Course Learning Outcomes

Upon successful completion of the course, students should be able to:

1. Use tensor notation to describe stress, strain, and constitutive behavior.
2. Derive governing equations and boundary conditions for elastic solids.
3. Formulate plane-stress and plane-strain boundary-value problems.
4. Apply stress-function and potential methods.
5. Analyze classical elasticity problems in Cartesian and polar coordinates.
6. Interpret analytical solutions in terms of physical behavior and limitations.

## Teaching and Learning Approach

The course combines clear presentation of fundamental principles with worked engineering examples, structured problem solving, and design or analysis activities appropriate to the subject. Emphasis is placed on connecting mathematical formulations to physical behavior, checking the reasonableness of results, and communicating engineering decisions clearly.

## Assessment Methods

- Problem sets and derivations
- Midterm examination
- Analytical assignments
- Final examination

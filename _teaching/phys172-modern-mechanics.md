## Course Overview

PHYS 172 is a calculus-based mechanics course emphasizing physical modeling, vector reasoning, Newtonian mechanics, and conservation principles. Students represent physical systems using diagrams, mathematical models, and computational or numerical reasoning where appropriate.

## Course Content

- Position, velocity, and acceleration
- Vectors and coordinate systems
- Newton's laws of motion
- Momentum and impulse
- Work and kinetic energy
- Potential energy and energy conservation
- Collisions and systems of particles
- Angular momentum and rotational motion
- Oscillations
- Gravitational interactions

## Course Learning Outcomes

Upon successful completion of the course, students should be able to:

1. Represent motion using vectors, graphs, and mathematical models.
2. Apply Newton's laws to particles and systems of particles.
3. Use momentum and energy methods to solve mechanics problems.
4. Analyze translational and rotational motion.
5. Apply conservation laws to physical systems.
6. Interpret mathematical results in terms of physical behavior.

## Teaching and Learning Approach

The course combines clear presentation of fundamental principles with worked engineering examples, structured problem solving, and design or analysis activities appropriate to the subject. Emphasis is placed on connecting mathematical formulations to physical behavior, checking the reasonableness of results, and communicating engineering decisions clearly.

## Assessment Methods

- Homework
- Quizzes
- Midterm examinations
- Conceptual and numerical problem solving
- Final examination

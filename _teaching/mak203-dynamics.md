## Course Overview

MAK 203 develops the kinematics and kinetics of particles and rigid bodies. The course applies force-acceleration, work-energy, and impulse-momentum methods to engineering systems in planar motion.

## Course Content

- Particle kinematics
- Normal and tangential coordinates
- Relative motion
- Particle kinetics
- Work and energy
- Impulse and momentum
- Planar rigid-body kinematics
- Planar rigid-body kinetics
- Mass moment of inertia
- Introduction to vibration

## Course Learning Outcomes

Upon successful completion of the course, students should be able to:

1. Describe particle and rigid-body motion using appropriate coordinates.
2. Apply Newton's second law to dynamic systems.
3. Use work-energy methods to solve kinetics problems.
4. Use impulse-momentum methods for transient motion.
5. Analyze planar rigid-body translation and rotation.
6. Select an efficient solution method for a given dynamics problem.

## Teaching and Learning Approach

The course combines clear presentation of fundamental principles with worked engineering examples, structured problem solving, and design or analysis activities appropriate to the subject. Emphasis is placed on connecting mathematical formulations to physical behavior, checking the reasonableness of results, and communicating engineering decisions clearly.

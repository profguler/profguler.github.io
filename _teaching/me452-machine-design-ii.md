Machine Design II is a senior-level undergraduate course that develops advanced knowledge of mechanical component and system design. Building upon the concepts introduced in Machine Design I, the course emphasizes fatigue analysis, mechanical power transmission, and the design of machine elements subjected to static and fluctuating loading. Students learn to integrate mechanics, material behavior, engineering standards, and design methodology to develop safe, reliable, and efficient mechanical systems.

## Course Topics

- Fatigue failure under fluctuating loading
- Fatigue design criteria
- Cumulative fatigue damage
- Shaft design
- Rolling-contact bearings
- Journal bearings
- Spur and helical gears
- Bevel and worm gears
- Gear trains
- Clutches and brakes
- Belt and chain drives
- Mechanical power-transmission systems

## Teaching Philosophy

The course combines engineering theory with practical design applications. Classroom lectures emphasize the derivation of design equations from fundamental mechanics, while numerous worked examples illustrate how engineering principles are applied in professional mechanical design practice. Students are encouraged to evaluate multiple design alternatives and to justify engineering decisions based on safety, manufacturability, reliability, and cost.

## Student Learning Outcomes

Upon successful completion of the course, students should be able to:

1. Apply fatigue failure theories in mechanical design.
2. Design shafts subjected to combined loading.
3. Select and analyze rolling-contact and journal bearings.
4. Design spur, helical, bevel, and worm gear systems.
5. Design clutches, brakes, belt drives, and chain drives.
6. Integrate individual machine elements into complete power-transmission systems.
7. Apply engineering standards and design methodologies to practical engineering problems.

## Assessment

Student learning is evaluated through:

- Homework assignments
- Design-oriented problem solving
- Quizzes
- Midterm examination
- Final examination

## Course Overview

ME 452 is an advanced machine-design course that develops the ability to design and select mechanical components subjected to static and fluctuating loading. The course integrates mechanics, fatigue, material behavior, engineering standards, catalog data, and design judgment in the analysis of power-transmission systems.

## Course Content

- Fatigue failure under fluctuating stresses
- Fatigue design criteria and stress concentration effects
- Cumulative fatigue damage
- Shaft design under combined loading
- Rolling-contact and journal bearings
- Spur, helical, bevel, and worm gears
- Gear trains and speed-reduction systems
- Clutches and brakes
- Belt and chain drives
- Integration of machine elements into power-transmission systems

## Course Learning Outcomes

Upon successful completion of the course, students should be able to:

1. Analyze machine components subjected to fluctuating and combined loading.
2. Apply appropriate fatigue-failure criteria and safety factors.
3. Design shafts and select bearings for specified operating conditions.
4. Analyze and design gear sets and gear trains.
5. Design or select clutches, brakes, belt drives, and chain drives.
6. Integrate machine elements into a safe and reliable mechanical system.

## Teaching and Learning Approach

The course combines clear presentation of fundamental principles with worked engineering examples, structured problem solving, and design or analysis activities appropriate to the subject. Emphasis is placed on connecting mathematical formulations to physical behavior, checking the reasonableness of results, and communicating engineering decisions clearly.

## Assessment Methods

- Homework and design-oriented problem sets
- Quizzes
- Midterm examination
- Component or system design activities
- Final examination

ME 452 develops advanced knowledge of machine-component and mechanical-system design. The course emphasizes the application of mechanics, fatigue analysis, failure theories, engineering standards, and design judgment to practical mechanical systems.

## Main Topics

- Fatigue failure and fluctuating stresses
- Cumulative fatigue damage
- Shaft design
- Rolling-contact bearings
- Journal bearings
- Spur, helical, bevel, and worm gears
- Gear-train design
- Clutches and brakes
- Belt and chain drives
- Design of mechanical power-transmission systems

## Teaching Approach

The course combines theoretical analysis with realistic machine-design problems. Students learn to translate loading conditions and functional requirements into safe, efficient, and manufacturable component designs.

Worked examples, design tables, standards, and computational tools are used to demonstrate the relationship between analytical calculations and professional engineering practice.

## Student Learning Outcomes

Upon successful completion of the course, students should be able to:

1. Analyze mechanical components subjected to fluctuating loads.
2. Apply fatigue-failure criteria in machine design.
3. Design shafts, bearings, gears, clutches, and brakes.
4. Select appropriate mechanical components using engineering catalogs.
5. Evaluate alternative designs based on safety and performance.
6. Integrate individual components into a mechanical system.

## Assessment Methods

- Homework assignments
- Design problems
- Quizzes
- Midterm examination
- Machine-design project
- Final examination

## Course Overview

CVL 231 introduces the structure, properties, testing, durability, and selection of engineering materials used in civil and mechanical applications. Emphasis is placed on linking composition and microstructure to mechanical behavior and engineering performance.

## Course Content

- Atomic bonding and material structure
- Stress-strain behavior and mechanical properties
- Material testing and interpretation of test data
- Metals and alloys
- Cement, aggregates, and concrete
- Asphalt and bituminous materials
- Timber
- Polymers and composites
- Durability and degradation
- Engineering material selection

## Course Learning Outcomes

Upon successful completion of the course, students should be able to:

1. Describe the structure and properties of common engineering materials.
2. Interpret stress-strain curves and material-test results.
3. Evaluate strength, stiffness, ductility, toughness, and durability.
4. Explain the behavior of metals, concrete, polymers, and composites.
5. Identify common degradation and failure mechanisms.
6. Select materials for engineering applications using appropriate criteria.

## Teaching and Learning Approach

The course combines clear presentation of fundamental principles with worked engineering examples, structured problem solving, and design or analysis activities appropriate to the subject. Emphasis is placed on connecting mathematical formulations to physical behavior, checking the reasonableness of results, and communicating engineering decisions clearly.

## Assessment Methods

- Laboratory or testing activities
- Quizzes
- Midterm examinations
- Final examination

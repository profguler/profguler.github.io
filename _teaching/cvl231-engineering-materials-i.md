CVL 231 introduces the structure, properties, testing, and engineering applications of construction and structural materials. The course emphasizes the relationship between material composition, internal structure, mechanical behavior, durability, and engineering performance.

## Main Topics

- Atomic bonding and material structure
- Mechanical properties of materials
- Stress-strain behavior
- Material testing
- Metals and alloys
- Cement and concrete
- Asphalt and bituminous materials
- Timber
- Polymers and composites
- Material degradation and durability
- Engineering-material selection

## Teaching Approach

The course connects material science concepts with civil and mechanical engineering applications. Experimental results, stress-strain curves, standards, and practical case studies are used to explain how material behavior influences structural performance and design.

## Student Learning Outcomes

Upon successful completion of the course, students should be able to:

1. Describe the structure and properties of common engineering materials.
2. Interpret stress-strain curves and material-test results.
3. Evaluate strength, stiffness, ductility, and toughness.
4. Explain the behavior of concrete, metals, polymers, and composites.
5. Identify common degradation and failure mechanisms.
6. Select materials for engineering applications.

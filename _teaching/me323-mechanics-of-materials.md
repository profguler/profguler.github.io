## Course Overview

ME 323 examines the behavior of deformable solids under axial, torsional, bending, shear, and combined loading. The course develops the ability to determine stress, strain, deformation, and stability limits and to connect these quantities to the safe design of engineering components.

## Course Content

- Stress, strain, and material behavior
- Axial loading and deformation
- Torsion of circular shafts
- Shear-force and bending-moment diagrams
- Flexural and transverse shear stresses
- Beam deflection
- Combined loading
- Stress and strain transformation
- Pressure vessels
- Column buckling and stability

## Course Learning Outcomes

Upon successful completion of the course, students should be able to:

1. Determine stresses and deformations under basic loading modes.
2. Construct shear-force and bending-moment diagrams.
3. Analyze beams, shafts, pressure vessels, and columns.
4. Apply stress- and strain-transformation relations.
5. Evaluate components subjected to combined loading.
6. Use mechanics principles to assess strength and stiffness.

## Teaching and Learning Approach

The course combines clear presentation of fundamental principles with worked engineering examples, structured problem solving, and design or analysis activities appropriate to the subject. Emphasis is placed on connecting mathematical formulations to physical behavior, checking the reasonableness of results, and communicating engineering decisions clearly.

ME 375 introduces students to the modeling, analysis, and simulation of dynamic engineering systems. The course develops the ability to represent mechanical, electrical, fluid, and thermal systems using mathematical models, transfer functions, and block diagrams.

## Main Topics

- Mathematical modeling of engineering systems
- Translational and rotational mechanical systems
- Electrical, fluid, and thermal system analogies
- Transfer functions
- Block-diagram construction and reduction
- First- and second-order system response
- Stability and transient-response analysis
- Introduction to feedback control
- Engineering-system simulation

## Teaching Approach

The course combines analytical derivations, engineering examples, and computational exercises. Emphasis is placed on connecting mathematical models to the physical behavior of engineering systems.

Worked examples are used to demonstrate how governing equations are developed from fundamental principles and transformed into forms suitable for analysis and simulation.

## Student Learning Outcomes

Upon successful completion of the course, students should be able to:

1. Develop mathematical models of engineering systems.
2. Derive transfer functions from governing differential equations.
3. Construct and simplify block diagrams.
4. Analyze first- and second-order system responses.
5. Evaluate system stability and performance.
6. Use computational tools to simulate dynamic systems.

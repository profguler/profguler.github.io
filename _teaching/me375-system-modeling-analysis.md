## Course Overview

ME 375 develops mathematical models of dynamic engineering systems and examines their response in the time and frequency domains. Mechanical, electrical, fluid, and thermal systems are represented using governing equations, transfer functions, block diagrams, and computational simulation.

## Course Content

- Modeling of translational and rotational mechanical systems
- Electrical, fluid, and thermal system analogies
- Linearization and governing differential equations
- Laplace transforms and transfer functions
- Block diagrams and block-diagram reduction
- First- and second-order system response
- Transient-response characteristics
- System stability and steady-state error
- Introduction to feedback control
- Computational simulation of dynamic systems

## Course Learning Outcomes

Upon successful completion of the course, students should be able to:

1. Develop governing equations for multidisciplinary engineering systems.
2. Derive transfer functions from differential-equation models.
3. Construct and reduce block-diagram representations.
4. Analyze first- and second-order transient responses.
5. Evaluate stability and basic performance characteristics.
6. Use computational tools to simulate and interpret system behavior.

## Teaching and Learning Approach

The course combines clear presentation of fundamental principles with worked engineering examples, structured problem solving, and design or analysis activities appropriate to the subject. Emphasis is placed on connecting mathematical formulations to physical behavior, checking the reasonableness of results, and communicating engineering decisions clearly.

## Assessment Methods

- Homework and modeling assignments
- Quizzes
- Midterm examination
- Computational simulation activities
- Final examination

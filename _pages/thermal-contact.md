<!--                    OVERVIEW                               -->
<!-- ========================================================= -->

<div class="research-section">

  <h2>Overview</h2>

  <p>
  My research in thermal contact resistance focuses on the coupled mechanical and thermal behavior of nominally flat rough surfaces brought into contact. Because real engineering surfaces touch only through discrete asperities, the true contact area is significantly smaller than the apparent contact area, producing an additional resistance to heat flow across the interface.
  </p>

  <p>
  This work combines rough-surface characterization, thermo-elastoplastic contact mechanics, finite element analysis, and multiscale heat-transfer modeling to quantify the effects of surface topography, material behavior, contact pressure, and thermal loading on interfacial heat transfer.
  </p>

</div>

<!--                    CURRENT RESEARCH                       -->
<!-- ========================================================= -->

<div class="research-section">

  <h2>Current Research</h2>

  <p>
  Current work investigates thermal contact resistance between rough copper surfaces using multiscale thermo-elastoplastic modeling. Numerically generated self-affine surfaces are characterized using parameters such as the Hurst exponent, roll-off wavelength, and spectral content, and are subjected to increasing mechanical load.
  </p>

  <p>
  The resulting true contact area, asperity deformation, interface pressure, and thermal conductance are evaluated to determine how roughness morphology and plastic deformation influence heat transfer. Ongoing work also includes finite element verification and the development of computationally efficient models for engineering applications.
  </p>

</div>

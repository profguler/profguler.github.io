<!--                     OVERVIEW                             -->
<!-- ========================================================= -->

<div class="cw-section">

  <h2>Overview</h2>

  <p>
    My research in crashworthiness focuses on the design and optimization of lightweight energy-absorbing structures capable of improving occupant safety while minimizing structural mass. By combining structural mechanics, nonlinear explicit finite element analysis, experimental validation, surrogate modeling, and optimization, I investigate the crash performance of thin-walled metallic, composite, foam-filled, hybrid, and additively manufactured structures subjected to impact or crushing loads.
  </p>

  <p>
    Particular emphasis is placed on multi-cell and corrugated crash boxes, progressive folding mechanisms, bio-inspired structural concepts, tapered tubes, foam-filled columns, vehicle crash systems, and simulation-driven design methodologies for automotive, railway, and protective applications.
  </p>

</div>

<!--                     REPRESENTATIVE CONTRIBUTIONS          -->
<!-- ========================================================= -->

<div class="cw-section">

  <h2>Representative Contributions</h2>

  <ul>
    <li>
      Developed and experimentally validated multi-cell, corrugated, foam-filled, bio-inspired, and hybrid crash-box concepts.
    </li>
    <li>
      Investigated progressive folding, peak crushing force, mean crushing force, specific energy absorption, and crush force efficiency under axial loading.
    </li>
    <li>
      Integrated nonlinear finite element simulations with response surfaces, Kriging models, machine learning, and multi-objective optimization.
    </li>
    <li>
      Studied the effects of cross-sectional topology, taper angle, wall thickness, foam filling, material selection, and geometric imperfections.
    </li>
    <li>
      Extended crashworthiness analysis to intercity-coach frontal impact, bus rollover, rail-vehicle absorbers, and occupant-protection systems.
    </li>
    <li>
      Developed simulation-driven methodologies for lightweight transportation structures and additive-manufactured energy absorbers.
    </li>
  </ul>

</div>

<!-- ========================================================= -->
<!--                     CURRENT RESEARCH                     -->
<!-- ========================================================= -->

<div class="cw-section">

  <h2>Current Research</h2>

  <p>
    Current research focuses on the development of next-generation corrugated and multi-cell crash boxes through the integration of nonlinear finite element analysis, machine-learning surrogate models, and multi-objective optimization. Current studies investigate how internal topology, corrugation geometry, wall thickness, taper angle, and material behavior influence specific energy absorption, peak crushing force, and crush force efficiency.
  </p>

  <p>
    Additional work addresses additively manufactured polymer and composite crash boxes, experimental validation, regression-guided design, and artificial-intelligence-assisted optimization. The objective is to accelerate the development of lightweight energy absorbers with stable folding modes, high energy absorption, and reduced force transmission.
  </p>

</div>

<div class="cw-section">

  <h2>Industrial Applications</h2>

  <p>
    This research is closely connected to industrial vehicle-safety applications. My experience includes intercity-coach frontal crash and rollover simulations, occupant-protection studies, crash-box development, bus passive-safety systems, and simulation-based design projects conducted with automotive manufacturers. These studies have involved LS-DYNA, nonlinear material models, contact, failure criteria, regulatory loading conditions, and experimental or full-scale validation.
  </p>

</div>

<!-- ========================================================= -->

<div class="cw-section">

  <h2>Future Directions</h2>

  <p>
    Future research will integrate artificial intelligence, digital engineering, advanced manufacturing, high-fidelity computational mechanics, and uncertainty-aware optimization to develop next-generation crashworthy lightweight structures. Particular emphasis will be placed on corrugated and hierarchical multi-cell concepts, 3D-printed energy absorbers, hybrid materials, experimentally validated machine-learning models, and crash systems for automotive, aerospace, railway, and protective applications.
  </p>

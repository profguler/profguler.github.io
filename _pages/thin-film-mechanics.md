<div class="tfm-section">
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>My research in thin-film mechanics focuses on the mechanical behavior, stability, and failure of film/substrate and multilayer systems at micro- and nanometer length scales. Particular emphasis is placed on interfacial stress transfer, buckling-driven delamination, graded bonding layers, cohesive-zone modeling, anisotropic substrates, and surface or microstructural size effects.</p>
<p>By integrating analytical mechanics, singular-integral-equation methods, fracture mechanics, and finite element verification, this research provides predictive methodologies for evaluating the reliability and structural integrity of advanced thin-film systems used in microelectronics, MEMS/NEMS, energy devices, coatings, sensors, and multifunctional materials.</p>
</div>

<div class="tfm-section">
<h2>Representative Contributions</h2>
<ul>
<li>Developed analytical formulations for interfacial stress transfer and delamination in orthotropic thin-film/substrate systems under mechanical and thermal loading.</li>
<li>Investigated couple-stress and surface-elasticity effects that become significant at micro- and nanometer length scales.</li>
<li>Developed cohesive-zone formulations for adhesively bonded piezoelectric actuators and progressive interface failure.</li>
<li>Formulated axisymmetric thin-film problems involving transversely isotropic and functionally graded substrates.</li>
<li>Studied double-film and multilayer systems to quantify the effects of film stiffness, thickness, and substrate geometry on interfacial stresses.</li>
<li>Validated analytical predictions through detailed finite element simulations and benchmark limiting cases.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="tfm-section">
<h2>Current Research</h2>
<p>Current work investigates the influence of surface elasticity on the buckling behavior of thin films bonded to compliant substrates containing interfacial delamination. Analytical formulations are being developed to quantify how residual surface tension and surface elastic properties alter critical buckling stress, delamination-tip behavior, and the subsequent instability of layered systems.</p>
<p>Ongoing studies extend these formulations toward piezoelectric and multifunctional films, multilayer structures, graded interfaces, and cohesive-zone models for progressive interface damage. Additional work addresses axisymmetric films, anisotropic substrates, and the coupling of mechanical, thermal, and electrical fields.</p>
</div>

<div class="tfm-section">
<h2>Collaborations</h2>
<p>This research has been carried out through collaborations with researchers specializing in analytical mechanics, fracture mechanics, generalized continua, piezoelectricity, graded materials, and thin-film reliability. These collaborations have supported the development of two- and three-dimensional interface models, cohesive-zone formulations, and size-dependent theories for advanced film/substrate systems.</p>
</div>

<div class="tfm-section">
<h2>Future Directions</h2>
<p>Future research will integrate surface elasticity, cohesive-zone modeling, multiphysics coupling, and data-assisted computational mechanics to improve the design and reliability assessment of advanced thin-film systems. Particular emphasis will be placed on buckling-driven delamination, flexible electronics, MEMS/NEMS, piezoelectric and thermoelectric devices, batteries, sensors, and multifunctional coatings.</p>

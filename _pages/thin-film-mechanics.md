<h2>Overview</h2>

<p>

My research in thin-film mechanics focuses on the mechanical behavior, stability, and failure of layered material systems at micro- and nanometer length scales. Particular emphasis is placed on buckling-driven delamination, interface stability, graded bonding layers, cohesive-zone modeling, and Gurtin–Murdoch surface elasticity.

</p>

<p>

By integrating analytical mechanics, finite element analysis, fracture mechanics, and computational modeling, this research provides predictive methodologies for evaluating the reliability and structural integrity of advanced thin-film systems used in microelectronics, MEMS/NEMS, energy storage devices, protective coatings, and multifunctional engineering materials.

</p>

</div>

<div class="research-section">

<h2>Current Research</h2>

<p>

Current work investigates the influence of surface elasticity on the buckling behavior of thin films bonded to compliant substrates containing interfacial delamination. Analytical formulations are being developed to quantify the influence of nanoscale surface properties on the critical buckling stress and the subsequent instability of layered systems.

</p>

<p>

Ongoing studies extend these formulations toward piezoelectric thin films, multilayer structures, graded interfaces, and cohesive-zone models for progressive interface damage and delamination propagation.

</p>

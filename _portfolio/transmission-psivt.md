## Project Overview

This industrial R&D project focused on the development of a novel **Power-Split Infinitely Variable Transmission (PS-IVT)** for agricultural tractors. The project was carried out in collaboration with **TürkTraktör A.Ş.** and **OTEST Automotive Test and Research Center**.

The primary objective was to develop a new hydro-mechanical power-split transmission architecture that combines continuously variable speed control with high mechanical efficiency. The project included conceptual transmission design, kinematic analysis, power-flow analysis, mechanical efficiency evaluation, optimization of transmission parameters, and fuel consumption assessment under realistic operating conditions.

A comprehensive computational framework was developed to model the transmission system, derive analytical kinematic and power-flow equations, predict transmission efficiency, and evaluate tractor fuel consumption using real operating data.

## Research Objectives

The project aimed to

- develop a novel power-split infinitely variable transmission architecture for agricultural tractors,
- derive the complete kinematic relationships of the transmission,
- formulate analytical power-flow equations for all operating modes,
- investigate transmission mechanical efficiency,
- optimize transmission parameters for improved efficiency,
- evaluate tractor fuel consumption using realistic driving cycles,
- compare the new transmission with conventional mechanical tractor transmissions.

---

## Technical Contributions

Major technical achievements include

- Developed a novel input-coupled power-split infinitely variable transmission architecture.
- Derived analytical kinematic equations for the planetary gear transmission.
- Formulated power-flow equations for hydraulic and mechanical power paths.
- Investigated the influence of Willis transmission ratios on transmission efficiency.
- Developed MATLAB software for transmission analysis and optimization.
- Evaluated transmission performance using realistic tractor operating data.
- Performed fuel consumption analysis based on GPS-recorded driving cycles.
- Demonstrated improved fuel economy compared with conventional mechanical transmissions.
- Established a complete analytical framework for power-split transmission design.

## Industrial Impact

The project successfully demonstrated the feasibility of a novel hydro-mechanical power-split transmission capable of improving the fuel economy of agricultural tractors while maintaining continuously variable operation and high driving comfort.

The developed transmission architecture combines mechanical and hydraulic power paths to achieve improved overall efficiency compared with conventional transmission systems. The analytical methodology developed during the project provides a general design framework for future generations of agricultural continuously variable transmissions.

---

## Project Impact

The project produced

- two SCI journal articles,
- one Ph.D. dissertation,
- a complete analytical formulation for a novel power-split transmission,
- MATLAB software for transmission performance prediction,
- computational tools for efficiency and fuel consumption analysis,
- a new transmission architecture applicable to agricultural machinery.

The methodologies developed during this project contributed to the design of next-generation high-efficiency tractor transmissions and demonstrated the advantages of power-split infinitely variable transmissions in reducing fuel consumption while maintaining excellent operational flexibility.

## Project Overview

This TÜBİTAK TEYDEB University–Industry Collaboration project focused on the development of an energy-efficient cylindrical roller bearing through advanced contact mechanics analysis, finite element modeling, numerical optimization, and experimental validation. The project was carried out jointly by TOBB University of Economics and Technology and **Ortadoğu Rulman Sanayi A.Ş. (ORS)**, one of Turkey's leading bearing manufacturers.

The primary objective was to redesign an existing industrial cylindrical roller bearing by minimizing frictional losses while maintaining fatigue life and load carrying capacity. The project combined computational contact mechanics, nonlinear finite element analysis, design optimization, laboratory testing, and statistical validation to develop an improved bearing suitable for industrial production.

## Research Objectives

The project aimed to

- design a new energy-efficient cylindrical roller bearing,
- reduce bearing friction moment without sacrificing fatigue life,
- develop detailed finite element models for rolling contact analysis,
- optimize roller crown geometry and raceway profile,
- validate numerical predictions using laboratory experiments,
- establish a computational design methodology suitable for industrial implementation.

---

## Technical Contributions

Major technical achievements include

- Developed detailed three-dimensional finite element models for rolling contact analysis.
- Performed nonlinear contact simulations of rollers, raceways, cage, and bearing assembly.
- Investigated the influence of roller crown height on friction moment and fatigue life.
- Optimized raceway camber geometry for improved bearing performance.
- Coupled numerical optimization with industrial design constraints.
- Designed and analyzed an improved cylindrical roller bearing geometry.
- Conducted friction moment experiments for validation of numerical predictions.
- Conducted fatigue life experiments using industrial bearing test machines.
- Performed statistical comparison between experimental measurements and finite element predictions.
- Demonstrated that optimized bearing geometry significantly reduces friction while maintaining fatigue performance.

## Industrial Collaboration

The project was conducted in close collaboration with **Ortadoğu Rulman Sanayi A.Ş. (ORS)** through the TÜBİTAK TEYDEB 1505 University–Industry Collaboration Program.

The industrial partner provided

- existing bearing designs,
- manufacturing constraints,
- experimental testing facilities,
- friction moment measurements,
- fatigue life testing,
- industrial design feedback.

The optimized bearing developed within the project was experimentally validated using industrial test facilities and compared directly with the baseline commercial bearing.

---

## Project Impact

This project demonstrated how advanced computational mechanics and optimization techniques can be integrated into industrial bearing design to improve energy efficiency without compromising durability.

The project produced

- one peer-reviewed journal article,
- advanced finite element models for rolling contact analysis,
- optimized roller and raceway geometries,
- experimentally validated bearing designs,
- an effective university–industry collaboration between TOBB ETÜ and ORS.

The numerical methodology developed during this project provides a general framework for the optimization of rolling element bearings and can be extended to other bearing configurations and tribological systems.

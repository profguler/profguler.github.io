## Project Overview

This TÜBİTAK-funded research project extended the computational peridynamics framework developed in the preceding project toward advanced fracture and delamination problems in composite structures. The research combined **Peridynamic Theory**, finite element implementation, analytical modeling, and experimental validation to investigate damage initiation and crack propagation under static, dynamic, Mode I, Mode II, and mixed-mode loading conditions.

Particular emphasis was placed on delamination in laminated composites, thick orthotropic plates, dynamic shear crack propagation, and composite structures containing geometric discontinuities such as holes. Peridynamic formulations were implemented primarily within **ABAQUS**, supported by MATLAB-based preprocessing and solution algorithms. The computational predictions were validated against finite element solutions, analytical results, published experimental data, and experiments conducted within the project.

---

## Highlights

- Extended bond-based Peridynamic Theory to fracture and delamination problems in composite structures.
- Developed a peridynamic formulation for **thick orthotropic Mindlin plates** including transverse shear deformation.
- Investigated Mode I, Mode II, and mixed-mode delamination in laminated composites.
- Developed bilinear peridynamic damage laws for progressive interface failure.
- Simulated sub-Rayleigh and intersonic Mode II crack propagation under impact loading.
- Implemented peridynamic formulations within **ABAQUS** and developed MATLAB preprocessing codes.
- Conducted experimental Double Cantilever Beam tests for Mode I delamination.
- Investigated open-hole composite specimens and interaction between geometric discontinuities.
- Validated peridynamic predictions against analytical, finite element, cohesive-zone, and experimental results.
- Produced three journal articles, three conference papers, one Ph.D. dissertation, and two M.S. theses.

## Experimental and Computational Contributions

The project combined computational development with experimental fracture mechanics. Major developments included

- MATLAB-based preprocessing tools for generating peridynamic interaction networks,
- implementation of peridynamic models in ABAQUS,
- bilinear constitutive laws for progressive peridynamic interface failure,
- thick-plate formulations incorporating transverse shear deformation,
- numerical simulation of dynamic Mode II crack propagation,
- Double Cantilever Beam experiments for Mode I delamination,
- comparisons with Cohesive Zone Modeling,
- analyses of open-hole composite laminates,
- investigation of delamination migration in multidirectional composites.

---

## Project Impact

This project represented the maturation of the Peridynamics research program initiated under TÜBİTAK Project 112M891. Whereas the earlier project established the fundamental computational framework, Project 115M585 extended these methodologies to experimentally validated composite fracture problems and produced a sustained body of peer-reviewed research.

The project resulted in

- three international journal articles,
- three international conference papers,
- one Ph.D. dissertation,
- two M.S. theses,
- experimental and computational methodologies for composite delamination,
- advanced Peridynamic formulations for thick plates, dynamic fracture, and mixed-mode loading.

The work demonstrated the capability of Peridynamic Theory to model complex fracture mechanisms in composite structures and established a foundation for continued research in nonlocal computational fracture mechanics.

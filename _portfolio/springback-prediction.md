## Project Overview

This TÜBİTAK-funded research project focused on the accurate prediction and robust optimization of springback in advanced high-strength dual phase steels during sheet metal forming operations. The project combined nonlinear finite element analysis, experimental validation, surrogate modeling, sensitivity analysis, and robust optimization techniques to improve dimensional accuracy and reduce springback variability in automotive sheet metal components.

The research addressed one of the major manufacturing challenges associated with advanced high-strength steels (AHSS), whose increased strength leads to significantly larger springback compared to conventional steels. The developed methodologies enabled accurate prediction of springback while simultaneously minimizing both the mean springback and its variation caused by uncertainties in material properties and manufacturing processes.

## Research Objectives

The project aimed to

- develop accurate finite element models for predicting springback in sheet metal forming,
- experimentally validate numerical predictions for multiple benchmark forming problems,
- investigate the influence of material constitutive models and process parameters,
- develop surrogate models for computationally efficient optimization,
- perform deterministic and robust optimization of springback,
- quantify part-to-part and batch-to-batch springback variability using probabilistic methods.

---

## Technical Contributions

Major technical achievements include

- Developed validated LS-DYNA finite element models for U-channel, 7-flange, and S-rail forming problems.
- Investigated the influence of anisotropic yield criteria including Hill48, Barlat89, and YLD2000.
- Performed extensive experimental validation of numerical springback predictions.
- Developed surrogate models using response surface methods, Kriging, radial basis functions, and polynomial regression.
- Proposed a computational framework for robust springback optimization.
- Developed a novel double-loop Monte Carlo simulation methodology for predicting both part-to-part and batch-to-batch springback variation.
- Investigated sensitivity of springback to material properties and manufacturing uncertainties.
- Demonstrated substantial reductions in both springback magnitude and variability through robust optimization.

## Industrial Collaboration

The project included extensive collaboration with **Coşkunöz Holding**, one of Turkey's leading automotive tooling and manufacturing companies. Experimental forming tools were designed and manufactured specifically for the project, enabling comprehensive validation of finite element simulations and optimization methodologies under industrially relevant conditions.

---

## Project Impact

The project established one of the first comprehensive research frameworks in Turkey integrating

- finite element simulation,
- experimental validation,
- surrogate modeling,
- uncertainty quantification,
- Monte Carlo simulation,
- sensitivity analysis,
- deterministic optimization,
- robust optimization

for springback prediction and compensation in advanced high-strength steels.

The methodologies developed in this project subsequently led to multiple journal publications, two M.S. theses, and laid the foundation for later research on robust design optimization and uncertainty quantification in computational mechanics.
